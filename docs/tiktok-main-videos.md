# Main TikTok videos

Same shape every day. A different real thought each time.

Each video has two jobs:

1. A real-life issue, said like it happened to you.
2. Angles, on screen, doing the actual cook.

Gear: two phones, CapCut, and a quiet room. One phone runs Angles and records the screen. The other stays outside the room and films you through a door or a curtain. A mic is worth using, because that camera is too far away to catch a quiet voice.

Target length: **25–40 seconds.** Portrait, 9:16.

## How it should look

The life shots are a look into the room, not a portrait. The camera phone is in the hall, or just outside a curtain. You are inside, in the chair, head to feet. The door edge or the curtain crosses the frame and hides part of you. You never look at the lens.

That frame is the whole style. It is the feeling of catching a private thought from the only angle a stranger gets. The app is the other angle, so those shots stay plain: bright screen, no curtain, no blur, no color filter. When the video cuts from the doorway to the card, the viewer steps from the room into the thought.

Leave the haze, the smoke, the heavy color, and the slow motion. A door left ajar is enough, and you can set it once and use it every day.

## The flow

| Beat | What the viewer sees | What they hear | About |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. The issue | You in the chair, seen through a door or a curtain | You say the thought in one breath | 4–6 s |
| 2. Into the app | The compose screen, already open, clear and bright | Nothing, or the end of the sentence | under 1 s |
| 3. Typing | Your thumbs, then the sent bubble | Nothing | 3–4 s after you speed it up |
| 4. Cooking | The cooking bubble, then a cut back to that same doorway as you look at the phone, then the card | Nothing, or a breath | 4–6 s on screen, even if the cook took longer |
| 5. The answers | The card. Tap each style chip. Hold the one you will talk about | Nothing yet | 8–12 s |
| 6. Your verdict | That same answer, held full screen | One or two sentences about a specific line, taken from the wide-shot audio | 5–7 s |
| 7. Save | Only when it earns it. Heavy thought: Private, then Save privately, or cut before the button. Lighter thought: Public is already on, tap Post | Nothing | 0–2 s |

No logo card. No intro. No close-up. The cut from the doorway to the composer is the transition.

## What you write before you film

Two lines, in Notes.

**Spoken line.** One sentence. A place, a number, or an object. This is beat 1.

**Typed line.** The same thought, shorter. One or two sentences. This is what you send in the app. Long paragraphs eat the video, and a vague line makes Angles ask several follow-ups.

Example for a work video:

- Spoken: “Thirteen months after I lost my job, I’m still in this chair, waiting to be rejected by something I haven’t even applied to.”
- Typed: `13 months after I lost my job, I'm still waiting to be rejected by something I haven't applied to.`

You cannot write the verdict yet. You write it after you see the answers.

## Gear, set up once

**Angles phone.** The phone with Angles installed and you signed in, with credits left. This one stays in your hand. Brightness all the way up. Do Not Disturb on, so a text does not land mid-cook. Low Power Mode off. Plug in if the battery is under 30%.

**Camera phone.** Any other phone you have. It only films you, and it never comes into the room. Prop it in the hall or just outside the curtain, vertical, on a shelf, a stack of books, or a chair. Use the back camera. It is sharper than the front.

Frame a little above your head down to your feet. The door edge or the curtain should take one side of the picture, about a quarter of the width. You sit in the clearer part. You should be big enough to read your posture. If the door fills the picture and you are a sliver, open it a hand wider.

Tap the screen on your body, not on the door. If the door is sharp and you are soft, tap again. Walk over, start the recording, go sit. Leave it running.

**Where you sit.** Inside the room, where the spoken line is true: the chair, the kitchen, the bed. The window lights the front of you. The hall where the camera sits can be dim. A ceiling lamp behind you makes a silhouette.

**Mic.** Use one if you have it. The camera is a few meters away, so the phone mic will sound distant. Plug the mic into the camera phone, or pair it as a headset, and clip it on your collar. The Camera app records it with the picture, so you never line up audio by hand. A separate recorder means syncing later. Skip that.

If you have no mic yet, move the camera phone a step closer and talk a little louder. Watch the first clip back. If you sound like you are in the next room, the next video needs the mic.

**Room tone.** Close the window if there is traffic. Turn off the TV, a fan, and the AC while the camera phone is recording you. You can turn them back on for the screen recording, because you will not use that audio.

## Film in this order

The verdict depends on the real answers, so the app comes first.

### 1. Screen recording

Add Screen Recording to Control Center if it is not there: Settings → Control Center → add Screen Recording.

1. Open Angles. Tap the sparkle so the composer is open and the keyboard is up.
2. Swipe into Control Center and hold the record button. Choose the microphone off if it offers a mic. You do not want room noise under the video. Wait for the red status bar, then wait three more seconds so you have a handle to trim.
3. Type the short line at a normal pace. Send it.
4. If Angles asks one question, answer it honestly. Tap a chip if one fits, or type a short reply. One question stays in the video. It shows how the app works.
5. If it keeps asking, or the question is off, stop this take. Close the composer without saving. Rewrite the typed line so it is more specific, and start the recording again. Do not fake a chip you would not pick.
6. Let the cooking bubble finish at real speed. Do not tap around.
7. When the card appears, pause two seconds on the first answer.
8. Tap the other style chips one at a time. Pause two seconds on each so the text is readable later. The chips are Stoic, Hopeful, Witty, and Tough. The card shows one answer at a time.
9. Come back to the answer you will talk about and hold it for five seconds.
10. Save, if this episode includes the button.
    - Heavy or private: tap the Public pill so it says Private, then tap Save privately.
    - Lighter, and you are fine with it on Home: leave Public on, tap Post.
    - If you are unsure, stop the recording before you tap. You can end the video on the answer.
11. Stop the recording from the red status bar. It lands in Photos.

Watch it once. Pick the line that actually did something, or the one that missed. Write the verdict in Notes, quoting a few words from that answer.

A verdict is one or two spoken sentences. It names the style and a phrase.

- Hit: “Tough said ‘you’re waiting for a no so you don’t have to hear a yes.’ That one sat me back.”
- Miss, still honest: “Hopeful tried to cheer me up. Stoic is the one that actually named it.”

Generic praise (“these are amazing”) plays as an ad. One hit, or one hit and one miss, is the point.

### 2. Doorway shot: the hook

Set the frame first. See the examples below if you are picking one. Then, on the camera phone, open Camera. Video. Back camera. Vertical. Prop it outside the room. Tap the screen on yourself so focus locks, then sit.

Say the spoken line once, to the room, while you look at the Angles phone in your hand or at the floor. You do not look into the lens. The door can hide part of your face. It should not cover your whole head for the entire line, or the shot becomes a door with a voice. Talk like you are telling one person, a bit louder than across a table, because the mic is on your collar or the phone is in the hall.

If you stumble, pause two seconds and say it again in the same recording. You will cut to the clean take. Three tries is enough. The first clean one is usually the most natural. Stay seated until you finish. Then walk over and stop the recording.

### 3. Same doorway: the cook and the verdict

Do not move the camera phone. The cut back during the cook has to match the opening.

**Watching the phone.** Sit, look down at the Angles phone, and stay there for about five seconds. You do not need the app open for this. The screen recording already has the real cook. This clip covers the wait.

**Verdict.** Still in that same doorway, say the sentence you wrote while you look at the phone. Stop talking. Hold still for one second so the trim has room.

In the edit, this verdict picture usually comes out. You keep the voice and play it over the answer, so the line you are quoting stays readable. AirDrop both clips to the phone where you will edit.

## Frames you can use

Pick one and leave it for the day. The hook and the look-down use the same frame.

**The hall door, left ajar.** Camera in the hallway, aimed through the gap. You in the chair inside, window on you. The door edge takes the left of the picture. This is the default. It fits the job-loss line, the email you keep refreshing, and any thought that happens in that chair.

**The bedroom curtain, not closed.** Camera on the window side of a half-drawn curtain, or just beside it. You on the bed, phone in your lap. The cloth crosses the lens and softens one edge. Use this for the account you checked before you got up, or the Sunday night that already feels like Monday.

**The kitchen door at night.** Camera in the dark hall. One lamp on over the table. You sit in that pool of light, seen through the doorway. The hall falls out of the picture as a dark band. Use this for the bill you said you could split, or the plans everyone made without you.

**The mirror past an open door.** Camera in the hall, the bathroom or bedroom door open, a mirror on the far wall catching you when a straight view would be a close portrait. You look at the phone, not at the mirror. Use this when the thought is about how you look to other people: the outfit for a call nobody will see, the message you reread three times.

**A second door, farther down the hall.** Camera one room back, so you are seen through two frames, smaller in the picture. Use it once in a while, when the thought is about being far from your own life. It is easy to make yourself too small. If you cannot tell you are sitting in a chair, move one doorway closer.

Check each setup on the camera phone before you record. You want four things: your whole seated body, a frame crossing one side, your body in focus, and enough of your face that a person can tell someone is there.

## Cut it in CapCut

1. New project. Set the ratio to **9:16** before you add clips.
2. Import four things: the doorway hook, the screen recording, the watching-phone clip, and the verdict clip. AirDrop them onto this phone first if you filmed on the other one.
3. Drop the hook on the timeline first. Trim the ends until the clip starts as you start the sentence and ends as you finish it. Drag the edge of the clip to trim. Play it. If there is a breath before the first word, cut the breath.

4. Put the screen recording after it. Trim the start so the first frame is the composer with your text, or the moment your thumb starts typing. The viewer should not see Control Center or the three-second wait.

5. Select the typing part. Speed → about **2×** or **3×**. The send tap should still be visible. If the typing is already short, leave it at 1×.

6. Keep the follow-up, if there was one, at normal speed. A chip tap is one second. Leave it.

7. The cooking bubble: if it is longer than about five seconds, cut in the doorway shot of you looking at the phone for three or four seconds, then return to the screen on the frame where the card appears. You can also set the bubble itself to **1.5×**. Land on the card at normal speed. The card arriving is the moment. Do not speed through it.

8. Trim the style section so each chip is about one second, except the answer you talk about. Leave that one long enough for the verdict.

9. Pinch the preview with two fingers and zoom in on the answer text until a person on a phone can read it. The full phone frame makes the type too small. Check the zoom on the chip row too, so the style name is still visible. If the zoom crops the chips, zoom out a little. Readable answer first, chips second.

10. Keep the card on screen for the verdict. Select the verdict clip, separate the audio if CapCut shows that, and delete the video track. The voice plays over the answer. The doorway already opened the video, so you do not need to appear again here. Leave the color alone. A film look, grain, or blur on the timeline will sit on the answer text too. The style is the door in the life shots. The card stays as the phone recorded it.

11. Captions. Captions → Auto captions, on the hook and the verdict only. Read every word. Fix the ones it missed. White text, dark shadow, near the lower third, above where TikTok puts its buttons. Do not caption the answers. They are already on the card.

12. Audio. Mute the screen recording. Keep the voice clips. If the room hiss is loud, try CapCut’s Reduce noise on those clips only, and stop if your voice starts to sound underwater.

13. Watch the whole thing with the sound on, once, on the phone, not pinched on a computer. You are checking three things: the first sentence makes sense with no context, the answer is readable, and the verdict quotes words that are actually on screen.

14. Export. 1080p, 30 fps. Save to Photos. Upload that file from the phone. TikTok’s own camera is a different crop. Do not re-record the screen of the CapCut preview.

## Example, cut by cut

Thought for the day: thirteen months out of work, still waiting for a rejection from something never applied to.

**0:00–0:05.** Hall door ajar. You in the chair, head to feet, the door edge cutting the left of the frame. “Thirteen months after I lost my job, I’m still in this chair, waiting to be rejected by something I haven’t even applied to.”

**0:05–0:08.** Screen. Thumbs type the short line, sped up. Thumb hits send. The dark bubble shows the text.

**0:08–0:13.** Cooking bubble for about a second. Cut back to the same door, you looking down at the phone. Cut back as the card lands. The cleaned thought is on the card, then the first answer.

**0:13–0:22.** Tap Hopeful, Witty, Stoic, about a second each. Stop on Tough and hold. Suppose that answer says you are waiting for a no so you never have to risk a yes. The zoom is tight enough to read that sentence.

**0:22–0:28.** Card stays. Your voice: “Tough said I’m waiting for a no so I don’t have to hear a yes. That one sat me back. Hopeful just tried to pat me on the head.”

**0:28–0:30.** You tap Private, then Save privately. Or you cut at 0:28 and skip the button. This thought does not need to be a Post.

The next day is the same timeline with a different spoken line, a different typed line, and sometimes a different door. The verdict is always filmed after that day’s cook. A Sunday-night thought can move to the bedroom curtain. A money thought at the table can use the dark hall into the kitchen. The edit does not change.

## When to show Post

Post is a real part of the app. Use it on days the thought is fine for strangers to read.

Leave the Public pill as it is (it starts on Public) and tap Post. You can add one extra second of Home, with the new card at the top, if the recording caught it.

On a heavy day, end on the verdict, or show Private and Save privately. Saving privately is also the product.

## A take you should not post

- The answers are generic and you have nothing true to say about them.
- Angles asked more than one follow-up and the thread is a maze.
- A notification, a name you did not mean to show, or a half-typed password is in the recording.
- The answer text is too small to read even after the zoom.
- The life shot is a straight portrait, or the door hides you so completely that the room looks empty.
- A blur or a color filter is sitting on the card.
- The thought is about hurting yourself or someone else. Angles stops and shows a crisis line. That is not a video.

Record another thought. The bad screen recording can stay in Photos.

## Day-of checklist

- Spoken line and typed line written.
- Signed in, credits left, brightness up, Do Not Disturb on.
- Screen recording with the mic off. One honest cook. Two seconds on each style. Five seconds on the one you will talk about.
- Verdict sentence written from the real answer.
- Camera phone outside the room, back camera, vertical. One frame only: door, curtain, or a farther doorway. You in focus, head to feet, the frame crossing one side. Hook, the look-down, and the verdict said in that shot. Mic on you if you have one.
- CapCut 9:16. Typing sped up. Cook covered with the same doorway if it runs long. Verdict voice kept, verdict picture removed. Answer zoomed until it is readable. No filter on the card. Screen audio muted. Auto captions checked.
- Watched once on the phone before you upload.
